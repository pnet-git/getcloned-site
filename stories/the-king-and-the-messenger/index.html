The King & The Messenger

By Akassh Ashok Gupta · 8 min read

Kings never delivered their own messages.

Think about it. When a King needed to send a decree to a distant kingdom, he didn't saddle up a horse and ride for weeks. He didn't spend 40% of his reign on the road, learning to be a better messenger. He appointed someone. A trusted advisor. A messenger. And that messenger became the vehicle for the King's words.

The King stayed in the palace. Making decisions. Building strategy. Leading the kingdom.

The messenger traveled. Carried the word. Delivered with authority and presence.

The moment a King grabbed a horse and started delivering his own messages, he stopped being King. He became a messenger.

The invisible cost isn't just time. It's opportunity cost. That's the real wound.
